Frequently asked questions about UK social benefits

  • What is Universal Credit and how much can I receive?

    Universal Credit (UC) is the UK's main means-tested benefit that replaced six legacy benefits (Income Support, Housing Benefit, Child Tax Credit, Working Tax Credit, income-based ESA and income-based JSA). In 2026, the standard allowance is £311.68/month for a single person under 25, and £393.45 for those 25 and over.

    You may also receive additional amounts for housing, children, disability, and caring responsibilities — bringing the total to around £678/month on average. Apply at gov.uk/universal-credit. You must be resident in the UK, aged 18+ (some 16-17 year olds qualify) and have savings below £16,000.

  • How to apply for housing benefit in the UK?

    Housing Benefit (for those in temporary accommodation or pension age) and the housing element of Universal Credit cover rent costs for low-income renters. Universal Credit housing element is based on Local Housing Allowance (LHA) rates set by the Valuation Office Agency for your area. For private renters, LHA typically covers the cheapest third of local rents.

    Apply for Universal Credit at gov.uk. If you're pension age, apply for Housing Benefit separately through your local council. Council Tax Support is also available separately from your local council.

  • What child benefits exist in the UK?

    Child Benefit is paid at £111.40/month for the first child and £73.85/month for each additional child (2026 rates). It is paid by HMRC and not means-tested, but subject to the High Income Child Benefit Charge if either parent earns over £60,000/year. Apply online at gov.uk/child-benefit.

    Additionally, the Child element of Universal Credit provides £333.33/month per child. Free School Meals are available for children in households earning below £7,400/year. Child Tax Credit remains in payment for existing claimants providing up to £3,235/year per child.
