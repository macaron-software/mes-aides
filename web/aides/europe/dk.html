Sociale ydelser Danmark 2026

Danmark har et nordisk flexicurity-system kombineret med høje skatter og generøse ydelser. Centrale ydelser: A-dagpenge, Kontanthjælp, Børnetilskud, Boligstøtte, Folkepension. Valuta: DKK.

DKK 14 500 Kontanthjælp

Hyppige spørgsmål om sociale ydelser i Danmark

  • What is A-dagpenge unemployment benefit in Denmark?

    A-dagpenge (unemployment benefit) in Denmark is administered through the A-kasse system (unemployment insurance funds). In 2026, the maximum benefit is DKK 19,992/month (90% of previous wage up to this maximum). You must have been a member of an A-kasse for at least 12 months and have worked at least 1,924 hours in the last 3 years.

    Benefits last up to 2 years. Denmark's 'flexicurity' model combines flexible hiring/firing with strong unemployment benefits and active labour market policies. Register at your A-kasse immediately after losing employment to avoid any waiting period.

  • How to apply for housing benefit (Boligstøtte) in Denmark?

    Boligstøtte (housing benefit) is administered by Udbetaling Danmark and available to tenants with low incomes. The maximum benefit is approximately DKK 3,000/month and depends on rent, income, household size and floor area. Both families with children and single adults aged 60+ or people with disabilities may qualify.

    Apply at borger.dk using MitID. Income must be below approximately DKK 200,000/year for a single person. The benefit is recalculated annually based on your tax return. If your income changes significantly during the year, you can request a reassessment.

  • What child benefits exist in Denmark?

    Denmark provides Børnetilskud (child allowance) of DKK 1,264 per quarter per child under 18, paid by Udbetaling Danmark. Single parents receive an additional supplement. Børne- og ungeydelse is universal and not means-tested — it is paid automatically once the child is registered in the CPR system.

    Additionally, Daginstitution subsidies cover 75–80% of childcare costs, significantly reducing the financial burden for families. SU (state education grant) of DKK 6,400/month supports students in recognised higher education programmes.
